GLOBAL · LAUNCH PREPARATION • Recommended plan

Your first session: learn, unlock, preserve options

For
A complete beginner
Before you begin
A created character and the opening tutorial

The first useful goal is control of your character and access to progression systems, rather than an expensive early item.

  1. Choose a class for the job you enjoy. Use the class comparison. Start with one main; the case for resource-farming alts depends on limits and binding rules that are still version-sensitive.
  2. Set up readable combat. Put dodge/defence, interrupt or stagger tools, healing and target selection on reachable keys. Enlarge party frames and enemy cast bars if the options allow. Adjust effects until ground telegraphs remain visible. Test camera sensitivity while moving.
  3. Follow campaign unlocks. Read tutorial prompts as new menus appear. Complete nearby side quests and Sealed Dungeons that show permanent progression rewards. A Sealed Dungeon is solo side content, not the same reward system as a repeatable group Expedition.
  4. Practice three things on ordinary enemies: move without losing your target, react to a visible attack cue, and recover with your defensive button. Learn flight and landing before relying on them near dangerous terrain.
  5. Keep choice boxes and scarce reroll resources unopened until you understand their contents. Equip useful quest gear, lock keeper items, and inspect an item’s Source menu before buying a replacement.

This is an editorial onboarding order based on the supplied progression notes and Kanon’s item-source advice. [U2] [U3] [C1]

Controls and platform notes

The spreadsheet reports V for flight, double Space for glide, / for targeting-mode changes and Alt + 1 for a travel action. Treat these as reported bindings: confirm your keyboard layout and client settings. Global is announced for PC through Steam and PURPLE; the official FAQ describes controller playability while fuller support remains under evaluation. [U3] [O2]

Common mistake

Copying a complex endgame hotbar before learning defensive cues, or spending every upgrade material as soon as a menu unlocks.

Beginner route: campaign plus permanent progress

For
Characters still leveling
Before you begin
Opening tutorial completed

Quest gear is temporary; system unlocks and appropriate permanent rewards make later progress smoother.

  1. Advance the campaign to the next useful unlock. When a new progression menu opens, inspect it once and assign available points. Use the client’s quest conditions rather than an old universal level schedule.
  2. Complete nearby permanent-reward side content. Preview Sealed Dungeon, regional quest and stronghold rewards. Favor skill/board progress you need. Mark unfinished locations for later rather than stopping the campaign for every collectible.
  3. Upgrade enough to play comfortably. Use obtainable gear and inexpensive improvements when damage or survival blocks progress. Save rare choice boxes and heavy rerolls for a piece with a longer life.
  4. Collect feathers on your route. Claim the actual Monolith reward shown. Do not expect the older Taiwan skill-point reward to appear in Global.
  5. At the current cap, finish available advancement quests and audit every system. Then enter the easiest useful group content whose actual requirements you meet. Learn it before buying a route into the hardest mode.

The supplied roadmaps use level 45 and a 1,500–2,400 “GS” ladder. They are useful historical priorities, not verified Global gates. KR/TW Chapter 1 raised the character cap to 50. This guide deliberately keeps the Global route tied to visible unlocks. [U3] [U4] [C2] [O6]

Midgame route: turn scattered upgrades into a build

For
Players at or near their current cap
Before you begin
Core systems unlocked and at least one repeatable activity available

Progress slows when resources are divided across too many temporary upgrades. Choose the next encounter and build toward it.

  1. Choose one next goal. Name the dungeon and difficulty, or the PvP mode, you want to play. Record its actual gate separately from your Combat Power.
  2. Find the limiting factor. Entry gate, survival, boss mechanics, low damage uptime and a missing skill breakpoint need different fixes. Use the diagnostic table.
  3. Complete cheap, predictable progress first. Spend unused board points, equip useful Arcana, finish a nearby permanent reward and review unlocked specializations before chasing rare gear.
  4. Concentrate investment. For damage, evaluate weapon improvement and key skill levels; for support, prioritize enough survival and the effects your assigned job needs. A meaningful skill line may be more useful than a color upgrade.
  5. Use a reliable farming activity. Compare useful rewards per successful clear and per scarce reward resource, including failure time. A harder dungeon is only efficient when you can complete it consistently.
  6. Move up after a stable clear. Confirm the next transfer plan, retain a workable old setup until safe to replace it, and reserve some currency for consumables and adjustments.

This spending order synthesizes Kanon’s gear/skill analysis with the supplied progression workbooks; it is a recommendation, not a proven fastest route. [U2] [U3] [U4]

Common mistake

Spending all currency to reach an entry number, then arriving without the survival, mechanics knowledge or functional skill setup needed to clear.

Skill levels: build toward a useful breakpoint

For
Players with the skill menu unlocked
Before you begin
Read the level and specialization panels for your chosen skills

An extra skill level is valuable when it improves a skill you use or unlocks a meaningful specialization.

Kanon distinguishes active skills you use, passives that modify your character, and Stigma skills with a separate upgrade path. Wisdom Stones raise the normal active/passive base levels in his documented system; equipment, Daevanion and Arcana can add further levels. [U2]

Normal active-skill level in the documentSpecialization effect
8First selection slot; first three options available.
12Second selection slot and fourth option.
16Fifth option becomes available.
20Third selection slot.

These are community-documented thresholds, not a promise that every launch skill or Stigma follows the same rules. Stigma perks in the document accumulate when unlocked rather than sharing the normal three-choice system. KR/TW Chapter 1 raises Stigma’s cap to 25; that does not confirm Global’s cap or available slots. [U2] [O6]

Recommended setup order

  1. Choose a small core: a main damage or healing skill, a survival tool and a mechanic tool. Read what their next breakpoint actually changes.
  2. Add up the levels from your current gear, boards and cards. Check the skill screen after equipping; do not rely on an item tooltip alone.
  3. Choose specializations for the encounter. More damage may be less valuable than movement, survival or an additional usable cast when learning.
  4. Recheck the preset after replacing gear. Losing one level can disable a specialization you built your sequence around.

Example, not a game requirement: if a skill sits at 19 and its level-20 option improves the job you are doing, a +1 line may be worth more than a generic rating increase. If the skill is rarely used, the same +1 may do little.

Common mistake

Assuming every class should force every skill to 20, or that an old build remains functional after removing a skill-level item.

Daevanion: spend points with a destination

For
Players earning board points
Before you begin
The relevant board and point type unlocked

Boards add stats and skill levels. Unspent or poorly routed points can be a cheaper fix than another gear roll.

The document describes eight boards. Nezekan, Zikel, Vaizel and Triniel share the initial point pool; Ariel, Azphel, Marchutan and Yustiel use other board-specific progression. Ariel is discussed as PvE-oriented and Azphel as PvP-oriented. Kodex reports that Ariel was unavailable in the EU test, so the regional eight-board layout is not a guaranteed launch menu. [U2] [C5]

  1. Identify which currency you received. Similar “point,” “crystal” and board names do not establish that they are interchangeable.
  2. Locate the useful skill/stat node before spending. Trace its required path and compare the total cost, not just the final square.
  3. Prioritize nodes that help your current role and key skill breakpoints. Fill productive nearby nodes as resources arrive.
  4. Inspect quests, Sealed Dungeons, achievements and item Source lists for the exact points you are missing. Record unfinished one-time rewards.
  5. Before changing a route, inspect the reset cost and refund behavior in your version.

A sheet’s claim that boards add “500+ combat power” is not a target or guarantee: the relevant gain depends on the version, nodes and rating scale. [U3]

Common mistake

Trying to reproduce an unavailable board, or selecting a longer route just because a screenshot has more filled squares.

Arcana: useful lines before expensive perfection

For
Players who have unlocked card slots
Before you begin
At least one obtainable card and a known skill goal

Cards can connect your gear to a skill breakpoint. Their usefulness comes from their effects, not only rarity.

Kanon’s early card family includes Chalice, Parchment, Compass, Bell and Mirror. Chalice can cover the broad skill pool; Parchment/Compass cover divisions of active skills and Bell/Mirror divisions of passives. Later regional content adds further card types and sets. Cards can carry Pantheon stats and other stat/skill lines, and his described card enhancement reaches +5 with random line growth. [U2]

  1. Fill available slots with useful cards rather than leaving them empty while waiting for a perfect set. Read both the card’s individual effects and its set conditions.
  2. Keep a card that completes a valuable skill breakpoint. Compare a replacement after equipping it, including the skill and set effects you would lose.
  3. Use low-cost placeholders while your target set is distant. Avoid consuming a potentially valuable card before checking its skill pool and future role.
  4. Budget for random enhancement. Do not assume feeding an old card into a replacement refunds every resource or all experience.
  5. Craft or buy a targeted card only when you know the eligible lines, the actual price and what improvement it gives your whole build.

Decision example: a lower-rarity card supporting your main healing or damage skill can beat a higher-rarity card with irrelevant lines. Conversely, a set bonus may outweigh a small isolated line. Inspect both; this is not a universal “ignore sets” rule.

Common mistake

Copying a ten-card Korean set into a Global plan before those slots, sets or sources are confirmed.

Pets and Genus: separate collection, riding and stat rolls

For
Players with pet progression unlocked
Before you begin
Inspect the pet and Genus menus

A pet’s appearance, riding utility, collection effect and Genus progression are different decisions.

The official Global overview describes pets as companions with riding and looting utility. Kanon describes the regional Genus system as five boards—Cogni, Fera, Natura, Varian and Special—whose Insight progression unlocks more lines and stronger eligible rolls. His reference has nine lines per board; Global’s line pools and limits may differ. [O3] [U2]

  1. Use a pet/mount you have unlocked and learn its available functions. Read owned and equipped effects separately.
  2. Progress Insight before chasing a perfect early roll. The document recommends reaching Insight 10 on regional boards before expensive optimization; verify that breakpoint in your client.
  3. When rolling, inspect which lines are locked, which can change and the cost. Locking more lines can make repeated attempts expensive.
  4. Keep affordable useful lines while progressing. Save screenshots of a working preset before major changes.

PvP fundamentals: survive together, commit together

For
Players learning coordinated PvP
Before you begin
A functioning preset and understanding of the objective

A coordinated group can make better use of its abilities than isolated players chasing separate targets.

  1. Enter with a plan. Identify the objective, the route back to allies and the teammate who calls targets. Review whether the mode changes your stats.
  2. Track pressure. Notice when your support is being attacked and when an opponent has used a major defensive tool. That information shapes the next push.
  3. Use range and terrain. Break enemy sight lines where possible without also leaving your own team unable to help.
  4. Commit deliberately. Combine a target call with available control and damage. If the target escapes or the objective changes, reassess instead of chasing indefinitely.
  5. Protect your response. Keep an escape or defensive option when entering danger; avoid spending every cooldown on the first exchange.
  6. Review one loss. Did you miss the objective, lose support, overextend or fail to answer control? Change the relevant habit before changing every gear line.

Use the class-specific role adjustments to apply this plan. These are editorial tactics grounded in the official class roles, not verified matchup win rates or claims about exact crowd-control immunities. [O5]
